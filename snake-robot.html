Undulating Gliding Snake Robot Dynamics

MATLAB/Simulink Multi-Body Simulation & Experimental Investigation

A team-based robotics project investigating how aerial lateral undulation influences the gliding performance of a bioinspired snake robot. The project combined a MATLAB/Simulink simulation with physical experiments to explore the relationship between undulation, aerodynamic behavior, and horizontal travel distance.

Research Question & Engineering Approach

Investigating the influence of lateral undulation on gliding performance.

Research Question

Flying snakes use lateral body undulation while gliding. This project explored whether introducing similar motion into a multi-segment robotic system could improve horizontal gliding distance.

The investigation considered how launch conditions and undulation parameters affected simulated flight behavior.

Engineering Approach

I developed a MATLAB/Simulink model using dynamics relationships and aerodynamic modeling approaches referenced from existing research.

The simulation was used to explore flight trajectories and the effects of undulation parameters. Physical experiments were conducted collaboratively to investigate the robot's behavior.

Simulation Development

Building and integrating a new simulation using established modeling methods.

My Technical Contribution

I was responsible for developing the MATLAB/Simulink simulation for the gliding snake robot. Starting without an existing snake-specific simulation, I used published dynamics models as technical references and AI-assisted coding to build and integrate the simulation components.

For visualization, I referenced an animation implementation from a separate academic gait-simulation example and adapted the approach to represent the multi-segment snake robot.

The resulting simulation provided a way to investigate how changes in motion parameters affected predicted gliding behavior.

Subsystem 01

Joint Motion Generation

A traveling-wave representation was used to describe lateral undulation across the robot's connected segments. Motion parameters such as amplitude and frequency were used to investigate different movement patterns.

Subsystem 02

Joint Actuation

A PD-based servo control block was integrated with the joint-angle command and multibody dynamics model to represent the robot's joint actuation within the simulation.

Subsystem 03

Dynamics & Aerodynamics

Existing research-based equations and aerodynamic relationships were incorporated to represent the motion of the connected segments during flight.

The model was used to examine the interaction between undulation, flight conditions, and predicted glide distance.

Subsystem 04

Kinematics & Visualization

The simulated segment positions were used to visualize the robot's motion and flight trajectory through a MATLAB-based animation.

The visualization approach was adapted from a separate gait-simulation example rather than an existing gliding-snake model.

Results & Engineering Insights

Findings reported from the team's simulation and experimental investigation.

Physical Experiment

Glide Distance

The team's reported experimental results at a launch speed of 1.8 m/s showed an increase in mean horizontal glide distance from 0.595 m to 0.647 m with undulation.

+8.7%

Reported increase in mean glide distance

Simulation

Undulation Amplitude

Simulation parameter studies investigated the influence of undulation amplitude on gliding performance.

The original project summary reported that an amplitude of approximately 10 degrees produced the greatest simulated glide distance among the evaluated settings under higher-speed conditions.

Engineering Interpretation

Increasing undulation does not necessarily improve performance indefinitely. Motion parameters must be evaluated alongside their aerodynamic effects.

Engineering Takeaways

This project provided experience building a dynamic simulation for a new robotic system by applying established research models and integrating multiple computational components.

It also reinforced the importance of interpreting simulation predictions alongside experimental observations, recognizing modeling assumptions, and evaluating how changes in motion parameters influence overall system performance.
