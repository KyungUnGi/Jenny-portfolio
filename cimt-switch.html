Biomedical Device Design

Constraint-Induced Movement Therapy (CIMT) Switch Device

Cerebral palsy often impairs motor control and causes involuntary curling in the non-dominant hand. Traditional CIMT relies on physically casting or binding the dominant arm, which causes sensory discomfort and resistance in young children[cite: 1, 2]. In partnership with clinical specialist Virginia Quellmalz-Zallocco, our team developed an engaging, non-restrictive switch system paired with an interactive toy to naturally encourage non-dominant hand use[cite: 1, 2, 3].

Device in Action

The child wears a soft magnetic strap on their dominant hand[cite: 1, 2]. Resting it over the switch triggers the internal Hall effect sensor, wirelessly activating the sensory toy[cite: 1, 2]. Removing the dominant hand immediately cuts power, motivating continuous therapeutic placement[cite: 1, 2].

Design Evolution

Prototype 1: Vertical Peg Switch

Initial tests used a vertical upright post[cite: 1, 2, 4]. Clinical input revealed that children with CP often experience hand spasticity that makes gripping cylindrical upright pegs uncomfortable or difficult[cite: 1, 2, 4].

Prototype 2 & 3: Ergonomic Mouse Shell

Pivoted to a gentle, mouse-curved enclosure[cite: 1, 6]. The form factor naturally accommodates palm resting without forcing finger extension, housing the magnetic sensor cleanly underneath[cite: 1, 2].

Sensory Toy Integration

CAD enclosure modeled for the master power interface

3D-printed protective cover installed directly on the busy board toy

To make the reward component safe and sturdy for a 3-year-old, the commercial busy board was modified with custom 3D-printed housing plates to protect wiring and keep the battery interface secure during active play[cite: 1, 2, 5].
