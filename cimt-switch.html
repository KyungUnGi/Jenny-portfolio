Assistive Device Design & Fabrication

CIMT Assistive Switch Device

Physical prototyping, CAD modeling, and iterative hardware fabrication of an assistive switch device designed to encourage therapeutic non-dominant hand usage in pediatric patients with cerebral palsy[cite: 1, 2].

CAD Modeling 3D Printing (PLA) Rapid Prototyping Hardware Modification Physical Testing

My Contributions

Physical Prototyping

Led the physical assembly and hands-on iteration of switch enclosures across each milestone, translating clinical feedback into 3D prints[cite: 2, 4, 6].

Custom CAD Integration

Designed custom 3D-printable housing components to mount and protect the master power switches directly onto the secondary sensory toy[cite: 1, 2, 5].

Needs & Gaps Analysis

Evaluated current clinical constraints—specifically how rigid casts and standard button switches fail to accommodate spastic hand curling in young children[cite: 1, 2].

Functional Assembly Demonstration

The final working system in operation: placing the dominant hand on the curved switch maintains sensor contact and delivers power to the sensory board, which shuts off as soon as the hand lifts[cite: 1, 2].

Form Factor Prototyping

Prototype 1

Vertical Post Switch

Fabricated an upright peg handle[cite: 1, 2, 4]. Testing revealed children with motor spasticity struggled to grip and hold cylindrical posts without excessive clenching[cite: 1, 2, 4].

Prototype 2 & 3

Ergonomic Mouse Dome

Redesigned into a curved resting dome[cite: 1, 6]. Allows the child to rest an open palm naturally while keeping the internal sensor properly engaged[cite: 1, 2].

Custom Toy Hardware Modification

CAD model designed to enclose the power toggle and secure internal wiring

3D-printed enclosure plate fitted and mounted directly onto the sensory board[cite: 1, 2, 5]

Modeled and 3D-printed a custom protective faceplate to adapt the off-the-shelf sensory toy, securely isolating electrical wiring and terminals for child safety[cite: 1, 2, 5].
