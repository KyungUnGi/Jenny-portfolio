Assistive Device Design & Fabrication

CIMT Assistive Switch Device

An Interactive Therapy Device for Children with Cerebral Palsy

Developed as part of a Carnegie Mellon University team project, this assistive device encourages the use of the non-dominant hand during play. A sensor-based handrest detects placement of the dominant hand and enables an interactive toy, creating an alternative to physically restricting the hand during constraint-induced movement therapy (CIMT).

CAD Modeling 3D Printing Rapid Prototyping Hardware Integration Design Iteration

Project Context

Team-based biomedical engineering project focused on pediatric rehabilitation and assistive technology.

My Contributions

CAD modeling, 3D-printed components, physical assembly, hardware modification, and iterative prototyping.

Project Outcome

Assembled a functional prototype demonstrating hand-position-dependent activation of an interactive toy.

Clinical Need & Design Objective

Translating a rehabilitation challenge into a physical device concept.

The Challenge

Constraint-induced movement therapy encourages the use of a less-used upper limb by limiting reliance on the dominant limb. Conventional approaches may involve casts or other restrictive devices, which can be uncomfortable or difficult to incorporate into play for young children.

The project explored a more engaging approach for children with cerebral palsy, including children who experience limited hand control or spasticity.

Our Design Approach

Instead of mechanically restraining the dominant hand, the system uses a handrest and sensor-based activation to encourage the child to keep that hand in place while interacting with a separate toy using the non-dominant hand.

The design emphasizes accessible hand placement, physical comfort, engaging interaction, and integration with an existing sensory toy.

Prototype Development & Design Iteration

Evolving the handrest geometry in response to functional requirements and project feedback.

Prototype 1

Vertical Post Switch

Initial Concept: Fabricated a vertical-post handrest intended to provide a simple physical interface for hand positioning.

Design Concern: Project feedback highlighted that gripping a cylindrical post could be difficult for children with limited hand control or spasticity.

Prototypes 2 & 3

Curved Ergonomic Handrest

Design Revision: Redesigned the switch into a curved handrest that allows the dominant hand to rest on the surface rather than requiring sustained gripping.

Engineering Rationale: The revised form factor was intended to accommodate a wider range of hand positions while maintaining sensor engagement.

Key Design Decision: The switch geometry shifted from a grip-dependent vertical post to a palm-supporting curved surface. This change addressed a potential accessibility limitation identified during the design process.

Custom Toy Hardware Integration

Designing and fabricating a protective enclosure for an existing interactive toy.

To integrate the switch system with an off-the-shelf sensory toy, I developed a custom 3D-printable enclosure component for the toy's power-control hardware. The design provided a mounting interface and helped cover exposed electrical components.

CAD Design

Modeled a custom faceplate to accommodate the power-control interface and provide mounting features for the existing toy.

Fabricated & Assembled Component

3D-printed the enclosure component and installed it onto the sensory toy, integrating the custom part with the existing hardware.

Engineering Considerations

The design required adapting a custom component to an existing product rather than creating the entire toy from scratch. Important considerations included component fit, physical mounting, access to controls, and reducing exposure to electrical connections.

Functional Prototype Demonstration

Demonstrating the integrated switch and toy system.

Hand-Position-Dependent Activation

The assembled prototype demonstrates the relationship between dominant-hand placement and toy activation. When the magnetic sensor detects the hand-positioning mechanism, the interactive toy is enabled. Removing the hand deactivates the toy.

Demonstration of prototype functionality; not a clinical efficacy study.

Evaluation & Verification Planning

Distinguishing demonstrated functionality from proposed performance testing.

Demonstrated / Documented

  • Fabricated multiple physical switch concepts.
  • Revised handrest geometry based on project feedback.
  • Created and installed a custom 3D-printed toy enclosure component.
  • Assembled a functional switch-to-toy activation system.
  • Documented a functional prototype demonstration.

Planned Verification

  • Drop Durability: Evaluate functionality after repeated drops from a 4 ft height.
  • Activation Distance: Measure the sensor's activation response as the magnet moves away.
  • Movement Stability: Assess whether the sensor remains active during lateral hand movement.
  • Usability: Collect structured feedback on comfort, ease of use, and engagement.

Verification procedures were proposed during the project. Quantitative results for these tests are not presented here.

Engineering Takeaways

This project demonstrated how mechanical design decisions must account for the abilities and constraints of the intended user. Developing multiple physical prototypes helped translate functional requirements and feedback into revised geometry and hardware integration.

The work also provided hands-on experience adapting custom CAD components to existing hardware, fabricating 3D-printed parts, and integrating mechanical and electrical elements into a functional assistive device prototype.
